Geodynamics · Rheology · Earthquake Physics · Active Tectonics

Department of Earth Sciences · University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa

I am completing my M.S. in Earth and Planetary Sciences (Geophysics & Tectonics) and am a DPST Royal Thai Government Scholar, fully funded for Ph.D. study. My M.S. thesis uses 3D numerical modeling (LaMEM) to study Galápagos plume–ridge interaction with power-law rheology. I am interested in the rheology of the lithosphere and mantle, earthquake physics and fault mechanics, and active tectonics in Southeast Asia, combining back-of-the-envelope theory, geophysical observations, and 2D/3D numerical simulations.

Research Interests

Bridging first-principles physics, geophysical observations, and numerical simulation in solid-Earth dynamics.

Lithosphere & Mantle Rheology

How the lithosphere and mantle deform, constrained jointly by numerical models and geophysical observations: power-law creep, dislocation vs. diffusion regimes, and viscosity–temperature–stress feedbacks.

Earthquake Physics & Fault Mechanics

Fault mechanics, earthquake-cycle processes, and what they imply for seismic hazard.

Geodesy, Seismology & Physics-Based Models

Integrating geodetic (GNSS/InSAR) and seismic observations with physics-based numerical modeling.

Active Tectonics of Southeast Asia

Active tectonics, stress and strain partitioning, and earthquake hazards across Southeast Asia.
